##### Introduction
*Microsoft* introduced a control that limits *Microsoft Graph* applications' and agents' access to *Teams* meeting transcripts, with the announcement *MC1393806*. This control is now active in tenants, disabled (*false*) by default : without administrator action, no application or agent connected through Graph can access transcripts.

---

##### Why this restriction
A Teams meeting transcript can contain extremely sensitive information : a meeting about a reorganization project, a discussion of compensation changes. Microsoft doesn't want agents to have free access to this content without explicit tenant approval, including to prevent transcripts from being processed by third-party connectors such as *ChatGPT* or *Claude* without authorization.

---

##### How to configure the control
The setting is configured from the *<u>Meeting settings</u>* section of the Microsoft Teams Admin Center, or via PowerShell with the *Set-CsTeamsMeetingConfiguration* cmdlet :
```powershell
Set-CsTeamsMeetingConfiguration -EnableAttributedTranscripts $true -EnableGraphTranscriptAccess $true
```

The *EnableGraphTranscriptAccess* parameter enables Graph access to transcripts. *EnableAttributedTranscripts* separately enables exporting speaker attribution (who said what during the call) ; this second parameter isn't required to enable Graph access and is currently only available through PowerShell, not from the Microsoft Teams Admin Center.

<Notice type="info">Both parameters were added to *Set-CsTeamsMeetingConfiguration* as far back as version 7.9.0 of the Teams PowerShell module. The Graph access control has therefore existed on the module side since July 2026 ; it's the switch to *false* by default across all tenants that is the subject of the MC1393806 announcement.</Notice>

---

##### The other prerequisites
Enabling the setting isn't enough on its own to grant access to transcripts. A Graph application must also meet two additional conditions :
- <u>Permissions</u> : Hold the *OnlineMeetings.Read.All* and *OnlineMeetings.Transcripts.Read.All* permissions.
- <u>Authentication and access policy</u> : Use app-only authentication with a registered application, itself authorized on the targeted user accounts through a *Teams application access policy*. The same requirement applies to any access to online meeting artifacts.

---

##### The JoinURL format trap
Retrieving a transcript depends on the *JoinURL* property to locate the corresponding online meeting. Teams has used two successive meeting URL formats :
- <u>Old format</u> : `https://teams.microsoft.com/l/meetup-join/19%3ameeting_` followed by a long value.
- <u>New format</u> : `https://teams.microsoft.com/meet/382314615403093?p=MhwpbLNlCVE8IvrANn`

Graph APIs only resolve the online meeting with the new format ; an attempt with the old format fails, even though the Teams client can still locate and open the meeting. Online meetings created after a tenant's rollout of the new join experience generally already use the new format.

<Notice type="warning">A script that works on recent meetings can fail silently on older meetings, simply because their JoinURL still uses the old format. Check the format before concluding there's a permissions problem.</Notice>

---

##### Conclusion
The Graph access control for transcripts closes a real blind spot : without it, any application otherwise correctly authorized could ingest the full content of a meeting. Three elements must now be aligned for an application to retrieve a transcript : the setting enabled on the tenant, the correct permissions and application access policy, and a JoinURL in the recent format.
