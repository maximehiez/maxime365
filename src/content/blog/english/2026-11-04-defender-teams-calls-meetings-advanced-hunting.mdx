##### Introduction
A user report says a *Microsoft Teams* call or meeting was suspicious, but it doesn't say what actually happened. To investigate, the security team needs the events themselves. Message center post *MC1532241* adds the *CallActivityEvents* advanced hunting table to *Microsoft Defender XDR*, covering Teams call and meeting activities. The feature is generally available : the rollout starts in early November 2026 and should finish by mid-November 2026, worldwide. No Roadmap ID has been announced.

Check the article on reporting security concerns in Teams meetings [HERE](https://maxime.hiez.ca/en/blog/2026-08-21-teams-report-security-concern-meetings).

---

##### What the table contains
*CallActivityEvents* describes the activities performed during your organization's Teams calls and meetings : who initiated them, who the organizer is, and whether the activity succeeded or failed. The key columns are :
- <u>ActivityTimestamp</u>, <u>CallId</u> and <u>ActivityId</u> : Date of the activity, and the call and activity identifiers.
- <u>ActivityType</u>, <u>ActivityStatus</u> and <u>ActivityDetail</u> : Type of the activity, success or failure, and detail.
- <u>CallType</u> and <u>CallSchedulingType</u> : One-to-one call (P2P) or group call (meeting, conference, escalated call), and scheduling (*Adhoc*, *Scheduled* or *Recurring*).
- <u>ThreadId</u> : Identifier of the conversation tied to the call.
<br/>

Initiators and recipients each have their own columns : ID, UPN, name, phone number, account type and home tenant, plus the tenant region and the platform (*Desktop*, *Mobile* or *Web*) for the initiator. The account type distinguishes *Entra*, consumer, *Azure Communication Services*, public switched telephone network (*PSTN*) and unauthenticated participants. The tenant, region and number help you spot external parties. The organizer has its own columns too (*OriginatorUpn*, *OriginatorObjectId*, etc.).

Here is a starter KQL query :
```kusto
CallActivityEvents
| where ActivityTimestamp > ago(7d)
| project ActivityTimestamp, ActivityType, ActivityStatus, ActivityInitiatorUpn,
          ActivityInitiatorAccountType, ActivityInitiatorTenantDisplayName, CallType, ThreadId
| sort by ActivityTimestamp desc
```

---

##### Linking calls to messages
The *ThreadId* column lets you join the table to *MessageEvents*, already available for Teams messages, to link a call to its conversation. *MessageEvents* only covers external conversations and internal messages that contain URLs. The data is also available through the event streaming API and the *Microsoft Sentinel* API.

<Notice type="note">In Sentinel, the table of the same name has a different schema from the advanced hunting one : *TimeGenerated* replaces *ActivityTimestamp*, and *OriginatorUserObjectId* replaces *OriginatorObjectId*. The two column lists can't be mixed in a single query.</Notice>

---

##### What admins need to know
- License : *Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 2*, included in *Microsoft 365 E5*. Plan 1 doesn't unlock this feature.
- Prerequisite : *Defender for Office 365* must be deployed in the Defender portal, otherwise queries return no results.
- Permissions : Analysts need advanced hunting access.
- Controls : No enablement setting or extra cost has been announced.
<br/>
The documentation already describes the table before the rollout is complete, so until mid-November 2026 it may not appear in your tenant. We recommend warning your SecOps teams, reviewing the schema, confirming analyst access and, if you use the streaming API or the *Microsoft Sentinel* API, checking ingestion.

The table contains UPNs, names, phone numbers and join links : access is best limited to the analysts who need it.

---

##### Conclusion
With meeting reporting, security teams get the user's alert on one side and the events to verify it on the other. It's worth preparing analyst access now, along with a few baseline queries on external parties, to test as soon as the table shows up in your tenant. One caveat : the documentation doesn't list the *ActivityType* values, so you will need to discover them in the data before writing filters.
